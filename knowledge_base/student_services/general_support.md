# General Student Support

## Department
Student Services

## Document Type
Student support knowledge article

## Purpose
Provide a safe first-line routing strategy when a student's request is broad, ambiguous, or spans multiple university services.

## Overview
General support is a routing layer, not a substitute for specialized knowledge. The Copilot should first identify what the student is trying to accomplish, then map the request to the best supported department or topic.

The primary supported domains in this knowledge base are IT Support, Academic Affairs, Finance, and Student Services.

## Common Questions

### I do not know which department handles my problem.
Ask what outcome the student is trying to achieve. Examples include logging in, resetting a password, enrolling in a course, resolving an exam-access issue, paying a fee, checking a refund, requesting a certificate, or correcting a record.

### My problem involves more than one department.
Identify the immediate blocker and route to the department that owns the decision or system involved. Technical symptoms may require IT Support even when the underlying action belongs to Finance or Academic Affairs.

### I need an answer that is not in the knowledge base.
Do not invent an answer. State that the available support knowledge does not establish the requested information and route the student for human confirmation.

### The student request is unclear.
Ask a focused clarification question rather than presenting a list of unrelated instructions.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

1. Identify the student's desired outcome.
2. Determine whether the request fits a supported topic.
3. If it fits, use the most specific knowledge article available.
4. If the request spans topics, identify the primary owner and any secondary dependency.
5. If the request is account-specific or requires an official decision, route it.
6. If the request is unsupported or ambiguous, ask for the minimum clarification needed.
7. If the knowledge source does not contain the answer, do not fill the gap with assumptions.

## Routing Guide

**Login, password, browser, portal, or system failure:** IT Support.

**Course enrollment, prerequisites, academic exceptions, exam decisions, or policy interpretation:** Academic Affairs.

**Fees, payments, deadlines, refunds, transaction verification:** Finance.

**Certificates, student records, general student-service requests:** Student Services.

When a technical issue blocks an academic or financial action, IT Support may own the technical incident while the academic/financial department owns the resulting decision.

## Requirements
Useful initial information:
- desired outcome;
- service or process involved;
- term or transaction context when relevant;
- exact error when technical;
- existing case/reference number.

Do not ask for passwords or unnecessary sensitive data.

## Troubleshooting

**Request is too broad:** ask what the student is trying to complete.

**Multiple departments appear relevant:** separate technical troubleshooting from policy/financial/academic decisions.

**No article matches:** do not guess. Route for human confirmation.

**Student asks for an exception:** identify the responsible authority rather than promising approval.

## Important Rules
The Copilot should prefer a correct escalation over a confident unsupported answer. It must not manufacture university contacts, phone numbers, email addresses, URLs, deadlines, fees, policy exceptions, or processing times.

When knowledge is insufficient, the correct system behavior is explicit uncertainty plus appropriate routing.

## When to Escalate
Escalate:
- unsupported requests;
- ambiguous requests that cannot be resolved with one focused clarification;
- sensitive/account-specific requests;
- official decisions or approvals;
- policy disputes;
- cases where the authoritative source is unavailable or conflicting.

## Information to Collect
Start with the student's desired outcome and collect only the minimum additional context required to route the issue.
