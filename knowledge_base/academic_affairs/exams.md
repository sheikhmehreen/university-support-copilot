# Exams

## Department
Academic Affairs

## Document Type
Student support knowledge article

## Purpose
Support students with general exam information and technical-access problems while preserving academic authority for assessment decisions.

## Overview
Exam-related support may involve schedules, assessment access, missing instructions, submission problems, technical incidents, or questions about what happens after an incident. The Copilot can provide procedural guidance when documented, but it must not determine grades, approve extensions, excuse an absence, or make an academic-integrity finding.

This article does not define actual exam dates, assessment rules, penalty periods, or accommodations.

## Common Questions

### I cannot access my online exam.
Determine the assessment and exact failure. Capture the error, time, browser/device, and whether the student can access other university services. If the issue is technical and urgent, route it to IT Support and/or the designated academic channel according to the documented process.

### I submitted an exam but I am not sure it went through.
Do not advise repeated submission without checking whether the platform provides a confirmation or submission status. Repeated submissions may create ambiguity. If status cannot be verified, escalate with the available reference information.

### I missed an exam because of a technical problem.
The Copilot may help document the incident and explain the relevant reporting channel. It must not promise a makeup, extension, grade adjustment, or penalty waiver.

### I need to know the exam schedule.
Provide only schedule information explicitly available in the current approved knowledge source. If the schedule is not present or may have changed, direct the student to the authoritative academic source.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

1. Identify course and assessment.
2. Determine whether the request is informational, technical, or a request for an academic decision.
3. For technical issues, capture exact error, time, browser/device, and action attempted.
4. Preserve any system-generated confirmation or incident reference.
5. Use the documented escalation route for urgent assessment incidents.
6. For requests involving extensions, makeup assessments, grading, accommodations, or academic integrity, route to authorized academic staff.
7. Do not promise an outcome.

## Requirements
Useful information:
- course;
- assessment name;
- scheduled date/time if known;
- exact error;
- approximate incident time;
- browser/device;
- whether the assessment was started;
- whether submission confirmation exists;
- support or incident reference.

Do not request exam answers, passwords, or unnecessary sensitive information.

## Troubleshooting

**Assessment page will not load:** refresh once, use a private window if appropriate, and try another supported browser. Record the time.

**Submission fails:** capture exact error and avoid repeated submissions if duplicate submissions are possible.

**Platform disconnects:** document the time and visible status. Reconnect only according to the platform's documented process.

**Student cannot determine whether submission succeeded:** look for authoritative confirmation rather than guessing.

## Important Rules
Academic decisions must remain with authorized staff. The Copilot must not decide whether a technical incident qualifies for an extension, whether a student violated assessment rules, or whether a penalty applies.

During a live or time-sensitive assessment incident, the Copilot should prioritize the documented urgent support channel instead of lengthy troubleshooting.

## When to Escalate
Escalate immediately or according to the documented urgent route for:
- live assessment access failures;
- submission failures;
- suspected assessment-platform outage;
- academic-integrity concerns;
- accommodation-related issues;
- extension or makeup requests;
- grading disputes;
- any case requiring an academic decision.

## Information to Collect
Course, assessment, exact symptom/error, time, environment, submission status, and reference numbers are usually sufficient for triage.
